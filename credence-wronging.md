# When Your Rational Credence Can Morally Wrong Others

**He Yuan**  
University of Massachusetts Amherst

## Abstract
Can one’s rational credence morally wrong others, and if so, would this generate a conflict between morality and epistemic rationality? I argue that it can, but that no conflict arises. First, in evidentially permissive cases where the same evidence permits multiple rational credences about one's intimate, holding a credence that casts them in a less favorable light can wrong them. Second, in evidence- gathering permissive cases where epistemic rationality permits either gathering or not gathering new evidence, choosing not to gather new evidence and thus maintaining a rational credence about one’s intimates that casts them in a less accurate light, can wrong them. Finally, I propose that in both kinds of cases, morality permits us to hold the most favorable credence within the epistemically permissible credence range, ensuring no conflict between morality and epistemic rationality. This proposal has advantages over competing proposals: unlike views denying doxastic wronging, it honors that intimates deserve doxastic partiality; unlike the Moral Encroachment Thesis, it avoids the challenge of specifying how moral encroachment applies to credence; and unlike the Epistemic Partiality Thesis, it offers a principled way to reconcile being doxastically more accurate with being doxastically more favorable toward one’s intimates.
